You are handing us photos of the inside of your home. This page says what we do to protect them, and — because a security page that only lists wins is not worth reading — what we have not built yet.

Report a vulnerability

Email contact@scmsolutions.org with “security” in the subject. Tell us what you found and how to reproduce it. We aim to acknowledge within 3 business days.

Research in good faith is welcome and we will not pursue legal action over it. Stay inside these lines: use only your own accounts and your own data, do not run automated scans or load tests against our backend, do not access, change, or keep anyone else's information, and give us a reasonable chance to fix an issue before you publish it. If you accidentally reach someone else's data, stop and tell us.

We are a small team and cannot pay bounties, but we will credit you by name if you would like that.

Your account

  • No passwords. Signing in sends a one-time code to your email. There is no password to reuse, leak, or guess, and we never store one.
  • Sign-in is handled by Supabase Auth, not by code we wrote. Codes expire, and the number of sign-in emails one address can request per hour is capped.
  • Sessions are checked server-side. Our backend functions verify your token against the auth service on every call rather than trusting what the browser claims to be.
  • The trade-off: whoever can read your email can sign in as you. Protect that inbox — it is the key to the account.

Your photos and saved spaces

  • Photo storage is a private bucket. It is not public, and nothing in it is reachable by URL guessing. Files are namespaced per user, and the storage rules only let an account read, write, or delete inside its own folder — enforced by the database, not by the app.
  • Every table has row-level security on. Profiles, spaces, media records: the policy is that a row is reachable only by the account that owns it. A bug in our frontend cannot hand you someone else's space, because the database refuses to return it.
  • Uploads are constrained to images (JPEG, PNG, WebP) with a size cap, so the bucket cannot be used as a general-purpose file host.
  • Guest use touches no database. Without an account, your answers and draft plan stay in your own browser. Photos go to the server for the analysis itself and are not stored afterward.

The AI calls

  • API keys never reach your browser. Analysis runs through our own server-side functions, which hold the credentials for Anthropic and Google. There is no key in the page source to lift.
  • Rate limits are enforced in the database, per hour and per day, in one atomic step so parallel requests cannot race past them. There is also a global daily ceiling that acts as a circuit breaker if something goes badly wrong.
  • Rate limiting does not require storing your IP. We keep a salted SHA-256 hash of it, and the salt rotates daily, so the hashes cannot be linked back to you or joined across days. Raw IP addresses are never written down.
  • Our backend only answers our own origins. Requests carry an origin allowlist, so another site cannot quietly spend your rate limit from a page you happen to be visiting.

Share links

A share link is an unguessable random identifier, and the payload behind it is built from an allowlist of fields rather than by removing the private ones. The direction matters: when we add a new field to a plan later, it is excluded from sharing by default, and someone has to deliberately add it. A share link never carries your photos, household details, progress, or shopping choices, and revoking one takes effect immediately.

Analytics and feedback

The usage events we collect are constrained by construction, not by policy: event names come from a fixed allowlist, properties must be flat primitives with hard length caps, and anything else is dropped before it is sent. Free text, photos, and household details cannot ride along even by accident. Do Not Track and Global Privacy Control turn the whole thing off. Feedback you type is written through a server function rather than by the browser, so the form cannot be used to write arbitrary rows into our database.

Infrastructure

  • The site is static and served over HTTPS by GitHub Pages. Backend calls go over HTTPS to Supabase.
  • The database, authentication, and file storage are Supabase's, running on AWS, with their encryption at rest and backups.
  • Secrets live in the backend's environment, never in the repository. The public repository holds no keys.
  • Schema changes ship as reviewed migrations, and the test suite runs on every change — including checks that the share payload stays minimal and that telemetry cannot carry personal data.

What we have not built

Being straight about this is more useful to you than a longer list above.

  • We have no SOC 2 report, no ISO certification, and no third-party penetration test. TidyMap is an early-access product from a small team.
  • There is no two-factor authentication beyond the email code itself, and no security-key or authenticator-app option yet.
  • We do not offer a paid bug bounty.
  • There is no in-app log of devices signed in to your account.
  • We depend on our providers — Supabase, GitHub, Anthropic, Google, Resend — and inherit both their protections and their outages.

If any of that is a dealbreaker for the space you had in mind, that is a reasonable call to make, and we would rather you made it with the facts.

If something goes wrong

If a breach ever affects your data, we will tell you directly by email — what happened, what was reached, and what to do — as promptly as we can and within the deadlines the law sets, rather than waiting for a quarterly update.
